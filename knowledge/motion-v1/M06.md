# Three.js and React Three Fiber: premium scene and glass recipe
DevKiller curated implementation playbook, 2026-09-23. Use only when the requested experience benefits from 3D. Confirm Three.js, Fiber and Drei are in the selected Engine profile; a retrieved recipe does not authorize a new dependency.

## Art-direct the scene before coding
Write a one-sentence scene brief with subject, silhouette, camera angle, background, light direction, material, motion and the page content it supports. Build one unmistakable focal object and a few supporting shapes, not a pile of primitives. Keep headline, navigation and actions in normal HTML. Choose a composition that still reads at phone width; provide a non-WebGL fallback.

## Glass must have something to reflect and transmit
Three.js `MeshPhysicalMaterial` documentation explicitly recommends an environment map for best results. A transmission value alone does not make a convincing glass object. Use a real environment map/HDRI or a controlled studio environment plus visible bright softbox-like reflection shapes, darker surroundings and an object/background behind the glass. Prefer a checked-in, licensed environment asset or a locally generated studio environment over an unverified runtime URL.

For a starting material, use `MeshPhysicalMaterial` with `transmission` near 1, `opacity: 1`, low but nonzero `roughness` (roughly 0.04–0.16), a physically plausible `ior` around 1.45–1.52, and modest `thickness`; tune against the scene, not in isolation. Keep a clear rim/highlight and enough separation from the background. Use restrained accent color on bevels or internal elements. A uniformly black, opaque-looking ball is a failed glass treatment: first verify environment lighting, exposure/tone mapping, camera angle, transmission and contrast; if it still reads poorly, switch to an intentionally reflective metal/acrylic treatment rather than claiming glass.

## Fiber scene recipe
Use a sized `<Canvas>` with responsive camera framing and capped `dpr`. Keep static geometry/materials stable; use refs and delta-based mutation in `useFrame` for small continuous motion—never React `setState` per frame. Share geometries/materials, avoid remounting the scene, and use demand rendering when nothing animates. Keep pointer tilt subtle and disable automatic orbit under reduced motion while retaining accessible controls.

## Visual acceptance gate
At desktop and mobile sizes, verify: recognizable silhouette at thumbnail scale; foreground/background depth; reflections visible on the intended glass; transmission visibly affects what is behind it; text and controls stay dominant/readable; no black blobs, clipping, z-fighting, flicker or excessive bloom; reduced-motion and WebGL fallbacks work. A successful build is not evidence of a successful material.
